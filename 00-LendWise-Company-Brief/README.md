## 1. Executive Summary

LendWise Financial Services Ltd. is a fictional European-headquartered fintech company operating across selected European Union markets and India.

LendWise provides digital lending and financial services to individual consumers and small businesses.

The company is progressively adopting Artificial Intelligence (AI) across credit assessment, fraud detection, customer support, document processing, recruitment, marketing and operational activities.

As its use of AI expands, LendWise intends to establish an enterprise AI Governance Program covering the full AI lifecycle, from use-case identification and risk assessment through deployment, monitoring, incident management and retirement.

This case study demonstrates the practical implementation of an AI Governance Program using synthetic scenarios and publicly available regulatory frameworks.

## 9. Credit Scoring AI

The LendWise credit-scoring system does not make the final lending decision.

The system produces a risk score using predictive modelling.

The current decision flow is:

Customer Application
→
Relevant Data
→
AI Credit Risk Model
→
Risk Score
→
Human Decision-Maker
→
Lending Decision

Human decision-makers remain responsible for the final lending decision.

The governance program will assess whether human involvement constitutes meaningful human oversight and whether decision-makers have sufficient information, authority and training to challenge or override AI-generated outputs.

---

## 10. Customer Support AI

The customer-support AI is designed to assist human support agents.

It can:

- Analyse customer queries
- Retrieve relevant customer information
- Access approved internal knowledge
- Generate draft responses
- Recommend potential support actions

A human support agent reviews the AI-generated response before it is sent to the customer.

The AI is therefore not currently authorised to independently send customer-facing responses.

Future expansion of the system's authority will require additional governance assessment.

## 13. Customer Emotion and Vulnerability AI

LendWise is considering AI capabilities that may help identify potentially vulnerable customers and support appropriate handling of sensitive customer interactions.

This capability requires careful governance assessment because emotion or vulnerability analysis may create significant ethical, privacy and regulatory concerns.

The use case will therefore be assessed separately from employee emotion analysis.

## 14. Employee Emotion Analysis AI

LendWise is considering a separate AI system for workforce-related purposes, including potential workforce-management and employee-retention insights.

This system will be treated as a separate AI use case because:

- The affected individuals are employees rather than customers.
- The intended purpose differs.
- The potential harms differ.
- The governance and regulatory assessment may differ.

No assumption is made at this stage that the system is appropriate for deployment.

## 16. Initial Governance Risk Areas

The initial LendWise assessment identifies the following broad governance risk areas:

### Customer and Personal Data

AI systems may process customer information including financial, transactional and account-related information.

### Internal Information

AI systems may have access to internal LendWise policies, procedures and knowledge bases.

### Third-Party AI

External AI vendors may process or interact with LendWise information.

### Data Leakage

AI systems could potentially expose sensitive customer or internal information through inappropriate outputs or system interactions.

### Excessive Access

AI systems or agents may receive access to information or tools beyond what is necessary for their intended purpose.

### Automated Decision Impact

Certain systems may influence or automate decisions affecting customers or employees.

### Bias and Fairness

AI systems used for credit, recruitment, fraud and other consequential activities may produce disparate outcomes.

### Human Oversight

Human involvement must be assessed for effectiveness rather than assumed to be sufficient merely because a human is present in the workflow.

### AI Agent Risk

Future autonomous systems may have the ability to perform actions through connected tools and therefore require additional controls.
