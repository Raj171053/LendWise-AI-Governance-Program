# LendWise — Data Subject Request Procedure for AI Systems

**Document status:** Proposed procedure — requires Legal and Privacy approval  
**Scope:** AI-001 Credit Scoring  
**Geographic scope:** European Union and India  
**Process owner:** Privacy / Data Protection function — appointment to be confirmed  
**Approval status:** Draft; not yet approved for operational use

## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how LendWise should receive, assess, investigate, respond to and document requests concerning personal data processed by AI-001.

It covers personal-data rights requests and distinguishes them from complaints or challenges concerning lending outcomes.

## 2. Request categories

- Access to personal data
- Correction or rectification of inaccurate data
- Erasure or deletion requests
- Requests concerning processing restrictions or objections, where applicable
- Questions about AI-assisted processing and relevant decision information
- Complaints or requests to reconsider a lending outcome
- Requests to correct information that may have affected an AI-generated credit score

## 3. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Request-handling team | Register requests, acknowledge receipt and coordinate the workflow |
| Privacy / Data Protection | Determine applicable rights, review legal requirements and oversee responses |
| Credit Risk | Investigate disputed credit inputs, scores and relevant lending records |
| Technology / Data Engineering | Locate relevant records and implement authorised data corrections or other technical actions |
| Legal / Compliance | Advise on legal obligations, exceptions, disputes and escalations |
| Authorised decision-maker | Approve responses or exceptions within documented delegated authority |

Named owners, deputies, escalation contacts and approval authority must be confirmed before operational use.

## 4. Standard workflow

### Step 1 — Receive and register

Record the request ID, receipt date, channel, request category, relevant jurisdiction and assigned owner. Do not record unnecessary sensitive information in the public governance repository.

### Step 2 — Verify identity proportionately

Apply an appropriate identity-verification process. Request only the additional information reasonably needed to verify identity and protect against unauthorised disclosure.

### Step 3 — Determine applicable requirements

Establish the relevant processing activity, legal entity and jurisdiction. Confirm the applicable legal basis, response deadline, permitted extensions, exemptions and any applicable phased commencement provisions.

Do not use one universal deadline for every request or jurisdiction.

### Step 4 — Locate relevant information

Search the appropriate systems for relevant personal data, input records, model outputs, decision records and associated information. Record the search scope and any systems that could not be searched.

### Step 5 — Assess the request

Determine what must be provided or changed under the applicable legal framework. Consider accuracy, retention obligations, applicable exceptions, confidentiality and third-party rights.

### Step 6 — Coordinate required actions

Where appropriate, refer disputed inputs to Credit Risk, technical corrections to Technology, and legal questions to Privacy or Legal. Determine whether corrected information requires a reassessment of the AI-generated score or a review of the lending outcome.

### Step 7 — Review and respond

Provide a clear, secure response through an appropriate channel. Explain any partial refusal or refusal where required, including applicable reasons and available complaint or appeal routes.

### Step 8 — Close and retain records

Record the response date, actions taken, rationale, approvals, unresolved issues and closure status. Retain the record according to the approved retention policy.

## 5. Request tracking fields

The internal register should include:

- Request ID
- Date received and channel
- Jurisdiction and applicable legal framework
- Request category
- Identity verification status
- Assigned owner and supporting teams
- Applicable deadline and any authorised extension
- Systems and records searched
- Assessment and decision rationale
- Technical or lending reassessment actions
- Response date and delivery method
- Escalation, complaint and closure status
- Evidence reference and authorised reviewer

Use synthetic records in portfolio demonstrations.

## 6. Escalation triggers

Escalate requests where:

- The applicable deadline or legal basis is unclear.
- Records cannot be located or data sources conflict.
- A correction may materially affect a credit score or lending outcome.
- A request involves a complex exemption, dispute or potential legal breach.
- The request reveals possible discrimination, unauthorised access or a broader model-control weakness.
- The response cannot be completed within the applicable timeframe.

The procedure must define actual escalation owners and service levels before production use.

## 7. Testing and assurance

Before operational approval, conduct authorised tests using synthetic cases. Include access, correction, erasure assessment, deadline tracking, identity verification, escalation and closure evidence.

For each test, record expected and observed behaviour, evidence, defects, remediation, retest results and independent review.

A written procedure alone does not demonstrate implementation or effectiveness.

## 8. Legal and governance limitations

This draft is not a determination that every listed right applies to every request. The GDPR and India's DPDP framework differ, and applicable requirements must be confirmed for the relevant processing activity and date.

Privacy and Legal must review the final procedure, jurisdiction-specific deadlines, exemptions, notices and retention requirements before implementation.
